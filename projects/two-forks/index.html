Product thinking.

Start from the problem the person arrived with. Four lenses, in the order I worked through them.

Nobody can pick dinner.

“Anything works” is the most expensive sentence in a group chat. The same person then vetoes seven restaurants, and dinner starts an hour late.

It is easier to swipe than to say.

Nobody names what they want, but everybody knows what they would swipe left on.

Reacting to a dish asks for no commitment out loud. A match score then turns a negotiation into a result that nobody had to argue for.

Three modes, one shareable card.

  • Duo, group, hot takes. Same swipe, three social situations.
  • Taste match. A percentage that two people can laugh about (34%, then 89%) is also the content.
  • A food personality card people want to post. That is the growth loop: share the card, a friend swipes, a new match appears.
  • End on the recipe. The app does not stop at the decision, it hands over the dish.

Swipes to first match.

The loop only works if the first match arrives fast and gets shared.

  • Swipes until the first match.
  • Share rate of the personality card.
  • Invites sent per session.
  • Solo versus duo versus group sessions.
  • For content: tags per meme (“tag the friend who says anything”).

How it reaches people.

The job is an audience that already knows the joke, and a link that gets opened.

Memes that get tagged

Ten days of teasers, rotating memes, carousels and reels, then a second week of image-only posts. Every post asks for a tag or a vote, because tags are how a new account reaches people who do not follow it yet.

meme→tag the friend→follow for launch day

  • Memes: the group chat, pineapple on pizza, “I'm easy, anything works”.
  • Carousels: how it works, the three modes, food red flags.
